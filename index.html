I build methods to evaluate, audit and govern AI systems, so organizations can deploy them with evidence that they work as intended. I also study human behavior on online platforms, with a focus on inclusion: how misinformation and hate speech spread, how different cultures and languages are represented or left out, and how people talk about AI.

My work is multidisciplinary. I collaborate with public health researchers, psychologists, linguists and Indigenous communities, and with public sector partners such as Brazil's Ministry of Health.

At UFMG, where I am a postdoctoral researcher, I proposed and lead FIAR, a framework for evidence-based AI governance applied to health AI projects. Before that, I spent seven years at IBM Research evaluating conversational agents, auditing machine learning models for bias and studying misinformation.

Selected results

  • FIAR, an evidence-based framework for AI governance, used at NIAR-Saúde to assess health AI projects in partnership with Brazil's Ministry of Health.
  • 16 granted US patents from IBM Research, including methods to evaluate conversational agents and to audit machine learning models for bias.
  • Technical lead at IBM Research on a Tech for Justice project on Black and tech communities online (ACM WebSci 2022).
  • Most cited work: misinformation in Brazilian WhatsApp groups (The Web Conference 2019, 300+ citations).
  • Honorable Mention at ACM CHI 2019, Best Paper at BraSNAM 2025 and Best Short Paper at IHC 2017.

Current work

  • LEME. A RAG assistant on Brazilian and international regulations for health and Responsible AI.
  • AI in public discourse. How AI is framed across languages and media, starting from YouTube in Brazil and the United States.
  • LLMs in psychotherapy. With psychology researchers, the limits of large language models when they engage with human subjectivity.
