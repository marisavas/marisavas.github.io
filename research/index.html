Research

My research sits at the intersection of technology and society. In social computing, I use large-scale data from online platforms to study human behavior: how information and misinformation spread, how hate speech targets social groups, which cultures and communities are represented or left out, and how people talk about AI. In Responsible AI, I develop methods to evaluate, audit and govern AI systems over time, so that institutions can show with evidence that their AI is used responsibly.

Responsible AI and AI governance

FIAR

FIAR (Framework for Institutional AI Responsibility) treats accountability as a continuous process. It combines a governance maturity model with evidence collected across the AI lifecycle, informed by LGPD, the EU AI Act and ISO/IEC 42001. I proposed and lead FIAR at UFMG, where we apply it to health AI projects at NIAR-Saúde, in partnership with Brazil's Ministry of Health.

  • FIAR-Saúde framework and audit templates on GitHub
  • From Principles to Longitudinal AI Governance. Data for Policy 2026, book of abstracts
  • Responsible AI for Public Health: a forecasting case study on SUS data. SBCAS 2026

LEME

LEME is a retrieval-augmented assistant that helps project teams find what Brazilian and international regulations say about health data and Responsible AI. I supervise its development at NIAR-Saúde.

  • LEME: assistente RAG em português para normas sobre saúde e IA Responsável. WebMedia 2026

Evaluation and auditing

At IBM Research, I created BotTester, a method to evaluate conversational agents with simulated users, and developed methods to audit machine learning models for bias. At UFMG, I study how well computational methods infer skin tone, as part of a fairness project on facial recognition.

  • BotTester: Testing Conversational Systems with Simulated Users. IHC 2017, Best Short Paper
  • Modeling Epistemological Principles for Bias Mitigation in AI Systems. AAAI/ACM AIES 2018
  • Performance Evaluation of Computational Approaches for Automatic Skin Tone Inference. LAAI-Ethics 2024

Social computing

Misinformation

With colleagues at UFMG, I studied how misinformation spreads in public WhatsApp groups in Brazil during elections and the COVID-19 pandemic, across text, audio, images and video.

  • (Mis)Information Dissemination in WhatsApp: Gathering, Analyzing and Countermeasures. The Web Conference 2019
  • Analyzing the Use of Audio Messages in WhatsApp Groups. The Web Conference 2020

Hate speech and underrepresentation

I study racist speech in Portuguese, the ties between Black and tech communities online, and gender gaps in STEM measured through advertising data.

  • Detecção de Discursos Racistas em Português na Rede Social X. BraSNAM 2025, Best Paper Award
  • Characterizing the Social Ties between Black and Tech Communities on Twitter. ACM WebSci 2022
  • From Inclusion to Contention: Analyzing DEI and "Woke" Narratives on Reddit. ASONAM 2025

Cultural representation

I look at how endangered Brazilian Indigenous languages are covered on Wikipedia, and at the opportunities and ethical limits of using AI to document and revitalize them.

  • Disappearing Without a Trace: Wikipedia Articles on Endangered Brazilian Indigenous Languages. ICWSM 2024
  • Balancing Social Impact, Opportunities, and Ethical Constraints of Using AI in the Documentation and Vitalization of Indigenous Languages. IJCAI 2023

AI in public discourse

I study how AI is framed in public discourse across languages and media, and which opinions large language models present on social questions.

  • Framing AI on YouTube: A Comparative Analysis of Discourse in Brazil and the United States. ACM WebSci 2026
  • AI and Climate Change Discourse: What Opinions Do Large Language Models Present?. ClimateNLP 2025
